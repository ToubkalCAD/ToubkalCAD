  "Healing pass" — explained simply

  When you draw a shape from separate pieces (a line, an arc, another line…),
  the ends are supposed to touch at the corners. But "touch" to a human eye and
  "touch" to the geometry kernel are different:

  - You see: the line and the arc meet at the corner. ✅
  - The kernel measures: they're actually 0.0003 mm apart. ❌ → "not closed" →
  can't make a face → can't extrude.

  To make a solid, the kernel needs a loop with zero gaps.

  I had two ways to give it a gapless loop:

  1. What I did now (faceting): Instead of using your real line+arc edges, I
  trace around the shape sampling lots of points and connect them with tiny
  straight segments — each point connects exactly to the next, so there are
  never any gaps. Guaranteed to work. The cost: curves turn into many little
  flat faces (the faceted look you see).

  2. Healing pass (the alternative): Keep your real edges (true line, true arc),
  but run a kernel tool (ShapeFix_Wire) that's allowed to nudge the endpoints 
  together to close those tiny gaps — like a tailor pinning two fabric edges so
  they meet perfectly before sewing. Result: the loop closes and the arc stays a
  true smooth curve, so the extruded solid has a genuinely round face instead
  of facets.

  Simple analogy: you have a circle drawn with a pencil but the two ends don't
  quite meet.
  - Faceting = trace over it with a ruler in many short straight strokes
  (closed, but now it's a polygon).
  - Healing = erase the tiny gap and join the two pencil ends (still a real
  circle).

  The trade-off: healing gives nicer geometry but it's a bit riskier to
  implement (it relies on more kernel functions, and this project has hit
  "hallucinated/wrong OCC API" issues before, per the notes). Faceting is
  bulletproof but lower-fidelity on curves.

  How it works — Ribbon ▸ Model ▸ Assembly ▸ Mate:
  1. Every visible solid lights up with pickable face overlays (extracted from
  the placed shape, so they sit exactly where each solid is drawn — even moved
  ones).

  are coplanar, touching, normals opposed, centroids aligned. Then it resets
  for the next mate.

  F_target · F_moving⁻¹ and applies it to the moving solid's current pose
  (THREE.Matrix4, decomposed to position + Euler + scale).

  OccFaceService extraction and the useCADSketchFacePick pattern), then
  updateTransform + cad-apply-transform to move the body, and rebuilds overlays

● Fixed. Here's the problem and the fix:

  What was wrong

  Your shape is 6 separate edges (Line/Arc/Line/Arc/Arc/Line) that visually form
  a closed loop but aren't a single closed wire. When you right-clicked the
  sketch and chose Extrude, the code only grabbed the first child edge — one
  open line — which can't make a face. Hence "wire must be closed and planar."

  The fix

  Extrude/Revolve on a sketch now auto-detects the enclosed region (the same
  Phase-18 logic behind the "Region" button) and extrudes that:

  - If the sketch is a single already-closed wire (e.g. one circle/rectangle) →
  uses it directly, as before.
  - Otherwise → runs region detection over all the child edges, picks the
  largest enclosed region, builds one closed profile wire (exact arcs + healing,
  faceted fallback if corners don't meet), and extrudes/revolves that.
  - If no closed region exists → clear message "This sketch has no closed region
  to extrude" instead of a cryptic failure.

  I also centralized the region-wire building
  (OccSketchService.buildRegionProfileWire) and toRegionEntity so the context
  menu, the Region button, and extrude all share one implementation.

  tsc + build are green.

  closed outline (the Extrude panel opens so you can set the height). Same for
  Revolve.
